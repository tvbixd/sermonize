# Scribe — Founder Video (you, to camera)

This is your talking-head script: you, speaking to the camera, telling the story
in your own voice. Different from `SUBMISSION_VIDEO.md` (that's the produced demo
with b-roll and voiceover). Use this one when the competition wants to see *the
person who built it*.

Written for **~90 seconds** (natural founder-pitch length). A **60-second cut** is
marked with ★. Say it in your own words — this is a script to internalize, not
read stiffly off a teleprompter. Where it says *[hold up phone]* or *[demo]*, do
the thing; showing beats telling.

---

## How to shoot it

- **You, framed chest-up**, looking into the lens (not off to the side). Good
  light on your face — a window in front of you is enough.
- **Phone or camera at eye level.** Don't shoot up your nose.
- **Quiet room**, phone close for audio, or a cheap lav mic.
- Talk to **one person**, not "an audience." Warm, not salesy.
- Keep the real **app in your hand** so you can flip to it for the demo beat.
- It's fine to **cut between takes** — do it in chunks, don't sweat one perfect run.

---

## The script (~90s)

### Open — who you are ★
> "Hi — I'm **[your name]**, and I built **Scribe**.
>
> I made it for a specific person: someone who stands up every week and preaches.
> My [pastor / dad / friend — pick what's true] would spend days preparing a
> sermon, deliver it once… and then have almost nothing clean to keep. Just a
> recording, if that."

*(If you'd rather not name a person, say: "I kept meeting preachers with the same
problem…")*

### The problem ★
> "Because here's what actually happens after a sermon. There's no structured
> record of what was said. Every scripture that got quoted has to be found and
> typed up by hand. And the person who's best at speaking the word ends up doing
> hours of the most tedious busywork just to save it."

### What it does — with the demo ★
> "So Scribe does the whole thing for you. Watch.
>
> *[hold up phone, tap record]* I tap record, and I preach. *[say a line with a
> verse]* '…and Paul tells us in Romans chapter 8, verse 28, that all things work
> together for good…'
>
> *[point at screen]* — and there it is. It heard the reference and pulled up the
> verse, live, while I'm still talking.
>
> *[tap stop]* And when I stop — *[show outline]* — the whole message is already
> organized. A title, the theme, a summary, my main points, and every scripture
> I cited, in my translation. I walk away with finished notes, not a wall of
> transcript."

### Why it matters / why me
> "What makes me proud of this isn't just that it works — it's that it works in
> the *real* room. I tested it in actual church services, and that's where the
> accuracy and the reliability came from. Not a lab. The pulpit.
>
> And I built it to be **free to preach with** — no subscription — because the
> people who need this most are all over the world, and I didn't want cost to be
> the thing that stops them."

### Close ★
> "It's live right now on iPhone and Android, it supports over two hundred Bible
> translations, and it keeps everything private, on your own device.
>
> It's called **Scribe**. The message you preached — finally kept.
>
> Thanks for watching."

## 60-second cut

Keep only the ★ beats and tighten:

1. **(0–10s)** "I'm [name], I built Scribe — for the person who preaches every week
   and keeps nothing clean afterward."
2. **(10–22s)** "No record of what was said, every verse typed up by hand — the
   best speakers stuck doing the worst busywork."
3. **(22–45s)** *[live demo]* tap record → speak a verse → it surfaces → tap stop →
   outline appears. "I preach, and it hands me back a finished, cited outline."
4. **(45–60s)** "Live on iPhone and Android, 200+ translations, private, free to
   preach with. Scribe — the message you preached, finally kept."

---

## Delivery notes

- **Slow down.** Nervous founders rush. Leave a full beat of silence after "and
  there it is" — let the demo land on screen.
- **Do the demo live if you can.** Judges trust a real tap-and-it-works far more
  than a slick edit. A small stumble is fine; it reads as honest.
- **The two moments that sell it:** the verse appearing while you talk, and the
  outline appearing when you stop. Frame the phone so the camera clearly catches
  both.
- **First and last lines matter most** — memorize those two cold, improvise the
  middle if you have to.
- **Be specific and true.** "I tested it in real church services" is your whole
  credibility. Don't reach for numbers or claims you can't stand behind — the
  honesty is the pitch.
- Fill in the brackets with what's real to you: your name, who you built it for,
  and a verse you're comfortable saying out loud.
